# BCBC 2024 — Division A, Part 1 Compliance
## Notes to Part 1

> Notes have **no legal effect** (per 1.1.3.1.), except Note A-Table 9.23.3.5.-B and Note A-9.23.13. of Division B.

---

## A-1.1.1.1.(3) — Factory-Constructed Buildings

The BCBC applies the same requirements to both site-built and factory-constructed buildings. Verifying compliance after delivery is difficult because wall, roof and floor assemblies are closed in.

**CSA A277** ("Procedure for certification of prefabricated buildings, modules, and panels") addresses this — an independent certification agency can review factory quality control procedures and make periodic unannounced inspections. A CSA A277 label provides assurance that concealed components comply.

**Important:** CSA Z240 MH Series ("Manufactured homes") standards contain requirements that **differ** from the BCBC. A Z240 MH Series label does **NOT** mean the house complies with the BCBC at its siting location.

The BCBC does reference **CSA Z240.10.1** ("Site preparation, foundation, and installation of buildings") for surface foundations.

---

## A-1.1.1.1.(5) — Heritage Buildings

The present BCBC was written primarily for new construction and provides a performance level significantly higher than what exists in many older buildings. Applying present Code provisions to existing heritage buildings is often impractical and may compromise historic appearances.

**Purpose of Table 1.1.1.1.(5):** The alternate compliance methods were developed to facilitate restoration and rehabilitation of heritage buildings without compromising Code objectives. Sprinklers are a primary method — they not only control fire and aid evacuation, but protect the building from destruction.

Only buildings **legally protected or officially recognized** by provincial or local government qualify as heritage buildings.

**Table 1.1.1.1.(5) is NOT mandatory.** An owner may choose to:

- Apply acceptable solutions in Division B
- Apply alternative solutions under Clause 1.2.1.1.(1)(b)
- Apply alternate compliance methods in Table 1.1.1.1.(5)
- Combine these options

---

## A-1.1.1.1.(6) — Alternate Compliance Methods for Alterations to Add a Secondary Suite

Division B requirements for secondary suites were written primarily for new construction. Applying them to existing buildings is often impractical.

**Table 1.1.1.1.(6) applies when:**

- Assessing an existing additional dwelling unit in a single-family dwelling (house)

**Table 1.1.1.1.(6) does NOT apply to:**

- New construction where no existing assemblies act as practical barriers
- Retroactive application to existing units

> **Figure A-1.1.1.1.(6):** Shows three scenarios:
> - LEFT (✓ APPLICABLE): New secondary suite added within existing single dwelling unit
> - MIDDLE/RIGHT (✗ NOT APPLICABLE): New secondary suite in a new addition, or new suite in a building with existing DU above/below (i.e., where the secondary suite and dwelling unit are stacked vertically in separate parts)

Table 1.1.1.1.(6) is NOT mandatory — same choices as heritage buildings above.

## A-1.1.1.2.(1) — Application to Existing Buildings

The BCBC is most often applied to existing or relocated buildings when:
- An owner wishes to rehabilitate a building, change its use, or build an addition
- An enforcement authority decrees that a building be altered for public safety

**Not intended** for retrospective application of new requirements to existing buildings, unless:
- Specifically required by local regulations or bylaws
- The AHJ has determined there is an inherent threat to occupant safety, OR
- Substantial changes or additions are being made, OR
- Occupancy has been changed

**Relocated buildings** can be considered as existing buildings in part. However:
- A change in occupancy may affect some requirements (loads, fire separations)
- Relocation to an area with different wind, snow or earthquake loads requires application of current code
- Reconstructed parts (foundations, basements) must be built to current codes

**Balancing act:** Applying Code requirements to existing construction requires balancing the cost of implementing a requirement with the relative importance of that requirement to overall Code objectives. Considerable judgment is required from both the designer and the AHJ.

## A-1.2.1.1.(1)(a) — Code Compliance via Acceptable Solutions

If a building design can be shown to meet all provisions of the applicable acceptable solutions in Division B, it is deemed to have **satisfied the objectives and functional statements linked to those provisions** — no need to consult Division A objectives to determine compliance.

---

## A-1.2.1.1.(1)(b) — Code Compliance via Alternative Solutions

Where a design differs from Division B acceptable solutions, it should be treated as an **"alternative solution."**

A proponent must demonstrate that the alternative solution:
- Addresses the **same issues** as the applicable acceptable solutions in Division B
- Will perform **as well as** (not just "well enough") a design that satisfies Division B

Division B establishes the **quantitative performance targets** that alternative solutions must meet.

**Level of Performance:**
- Where Division B offers a choice among designs with varying performance levels, the **lowest level** among them establishes the minimum acceptable performance for evaluating alternative solutions
- Where a single design is an alternative to several sets of acceptable solutions, it must be equivalent to the **overall level of performance** of all applicable sets taken as a whole

**Areas of Performance:**
- Alternative solutions only need to meet performance areas defined by the objectives and functional statements **attributed to the applicable acceptable solutions**
- If no applicable acceptable solutions are linked to a particular objective, alternative solutions do not need to address that objective

---

## A-1.2.1.2.(1) — Responsibility of Owner

Sentence 1.2.1.2.(1) is **not intended to imply** that a new owner must bring the entire building into compliance with the Code. The Code applies only in the cases and to the extent specified by Article 1.1.1.1.

If none of the provisions in 1.1.1.1.(1) apply, the owner is **not required** to make any changes.

## A-1.3.3.4.(1) — Buildings Divided by Firewalls

The concept of treating firewall-divided portions as separate buildings relates only to provisions **directly regulated by this Code**, and does **not apply** to electrical service entrance requirements (regulated by other documents).

---

## A-1.3.3.4.(2) — Buildings on Sloping Sites

Applying the definition of *grade* to stepped buildings on sloping sites often results in buildings being designated as more than 4 storeys even though there may be only 2, 3 or 4 storeys at any one location.

> **Figure A-1.3.3.4.(2) — Application of the definition of grade:**
>
> ```
> Building A (stepped, sloping site):
> Grade is measured at each stepped portion independently.
> ┌─────┐
> │ ███ │  ← each portion has own grade level
>     │ ███ │
>         │ ███ │
> ▼▼▼  ▼▼▼  ▼▼▼  (grade for each stepped portion)
>
> Building B (flat site):
> Single grade level for entire building.
> ┌──────────────┐
> │ ███ ███ ███  │
> ▼▼▼▼▼▼▼▼▼▼▼▼  (single grade for entire building)
> ```
>
> Building A can be considered as **4 storeys** in building height under Sentence 1.3.3.4.(2) instead of 7 storeys. Both Building A and B are comparable for fire safety and egress.

This relaxation applies to **building height determination only** — all other requirements continue to apply.

## A-1.4.1.2.(1) — Defined Terms (Explanatory Notes)

### ASTC and STC

- Higher ASTC or STC rating = better protection from noise in adjacent spaces
- Ratings are determined per **ASTM E413** ("Classification for Rating Sound Insulation")
- **STC** = direct sound transmission only
- **ASTC** = direct + flanking transmission paths (more comprehensive)

### Care Occupancy

"Support services rendered by or through care facility management" = services provided by the **organization responsible for care for a period exceeding 24 consecutive hours**.

Does NOT include services arranged directly by residents themselves with outside agencies.

Examples of care services: daily assessment, awareness of whereabouts, appointment management, crisis intervention, nutrition/medication supervision, activities of daily living (bathing, dressing, feeding).

No actual **treatment** is provided by or through care facility management.

### Dangerous Goods

BCBC uses a combined TDGR/WHMIS approach. See **Table A-1.4.1.2.(1)** for mapping between TDGR, WHMIS, and BCBC class descriptors.

| TDGR Class | BCBC Descriptor |
|-----------|----------------|
| 1 | Explosives |
| 2.1 | Flammable gases; Flammable aerosols |
| 2.2 | Non-flammable, non-toxic gases |
| 2.3 | Toxic gases |
| 3 | Flammable liquids |
| 4.1 | Flammable solids |
| 4.2 | Pyrophoric materials |
| 4.3 | Water-reactive substances |
| 5.1 | Oxidizers |
| 5.2 | Organic peroxides |
| 6.1 | Toxic substances |
| 6.2 | Infectious materials |
| 7 | Radioactive materials |
| 8 | Corrosives |
| 9 | Miscellaneous dangerous goods |
| — | Dangerously reactive materials |

### Exit

Exits include:
- Doors or doorways leading directly into an exit stair or directly to the outside
- Where exit leads to a separate building: vestibules, walkways, bridges or balconies

### Farm Building

Farm buildings include (but not limited to): produce storage/packing, livestock/poultry housing, milking centres, manure storage, grain bins, silos, feed preparation, farm workshops, greenhouses, farm retail centres, horse riding/exercise/training facilities.

May be classified as **low or high human occupancy** depending on occupant load.

### Fire Separation

"Fire" refers to all products of combustion, including heat and smoke. A fire separation is not always required to have a fire-resistance rating, but should act as a barrier to smoke and fire until some response is initiated.

**Key principle:** If the FRR of a fire separation is waived based on automatic sprinklers, it is still the intent of the Code that the fire separation remain in place and act as a barrier against smoke until the sprinklers have actuated.

### Flight (Stair)

> **Figure A-1.4.1.2.(1)-A — Flight:**
>
> ```
> Stair with ONE flight:        Stair with TWO flights:
>
>    landing                      landing    landing
>      ▲                            ▲     ▲
>      |  flight                    | f1  | f2
>      |  /////                     | //  | //
>   landing                      landing    landing
> ```
>
> A **flight** = series of steps between landings.

### Grade

Localized depressions that need **not** be considered in determining grade elevation include: vehicle and pedestrian entrances and other minor depressions that do not affect accessibility for firefighting or evacuation.

### Mechanically Vented

Includes all types of appliances/venting systems that rely entirely on fans to evacuate products of combustion ("forced draft," "power vented," "induced draft"). Key characteristic: more resistant to depressurization-induced spillage because the combustion venting system downstream of the fan is **sealed** (no draft hood or draft control device).

### Post-Disaster Building

AHJ may choose to **exempt** certain buildings (e.g., a volunteer paramedic's residence where an ambulance is stored, or a police station in a shopping mall). Municipalities with emergency management plans should coordinate with provincial/territorial emergency management protocols.

Buildings with **rooftop telecommunication or cellular network equipment** need not be designated as post-disaster buildings.

### Public Corridor

A **covered mall** is considered to be a public corridor and is subject to the same requirements.

### Rim Joist

In the field, rim joists may also be referred to as **rim boards, headers or header joists**.

### Secondary Suite

A secondary suite is typically created within an existing single dwelling unit ("house") — either as an addition, an alteration, or incorporated during new construction.

**Key points:**
- A secondary suite may have more than one storey
- May be on the same level as the other dwelling unit, or above or below it
- Permitted in: individual detached houses, semi-detached houses (side-by-side), freehold row houses with vertical fire separation
- **NOT permitted in:** apartment buildings (dwelling units share horizontal assemblies above/below)
- Neither unit can be strata-titled or subdivided from the remainder of the house

> **Figure A-1.4.1.2.(1)-C — Building Types where Secondary Suites are Permitted:**
>
> ```
> PERMITTED (✓):
> ┌────┬────┬────┬────┐  (detached houses, semi-detached, row houses)
> │ DU │ DU │ DU │ DU │  — each with own vertical fire separation
> │ SS │ SS │ SS │ SS │
> └────┴────┴────┴────┘
>
> NOT PERMITTED (✗):
> ┌─────────────────────┐
> │   DU or OTHER OCC   │  ← dwelling units or other occupancy
> ├─────────────────────┤     above/below (shared horizontal assembly)
> │   ✗✗  DU  ✗✗  DU   │
> └─────────────────────┘
> ```

### Service Room

Typical examples: boiler rooms, furnace rooms, incinerator rooms, garbage handling rooms, rooms for air-conditioning or heating appliances, pumps, compressors and electrical equipment.

**Not service rooms:** elevator machine rooms, common laundry rooms.

### Solid Masonry Units

Net solid area = gross area of bed face (L × T) minus cumulative hollow portions. If hollow portions ≤ 25% of gross area → solid masonry unit.

> **Figure A-1.4.1.2.(1)-B — Net solid area of masonry unit:**
> - gross area = length × thickness (bed face)
> - net solid area = gross area minus hollow portions
> - ≥ 75% solid = qualifies as solid masonry unit

### Suite

"Tenancy" applies to both rental and ownership tenure (e.g., condo dwelling units are separate suites even though individually owned). A series of rooms constituting a suite must be in reasonably close proximity and have access to each other directly or indirectly.

"Room or suite" in the Code (e.g., travel distance) = requirement applies within the rooms of suites AND to rooms outside suites.

"Suite, and rooms not located within a suite" (e.g., smoke/heat detectors) = applies to individual suites but NOT to each room within the suite; rooms "not within a suite" include common laundry rooms, common recreational rooms and service rooms.

### Tapered Tread

Includes treads in **curved stairs** and **winder stairs** (though requirements for winders differ from other tapered treads).

### Treatment

"Ability to evacuate unassisted" = person can recognize and respond to an emergency AND move to a safe location without assistance — must be able to arise and walk, or transfer from bed/chair to a means of mobility, and leave the building independently.

### Treatment Occupancy

"Treatments" include: surgery, intensive care, emergency medical intervention.

Treatment differs from:
- Care occupancy services (personal care assistance, medication administration)
- Business/personal services occupancy (dentistry, day procedures)

### Vapour Barrier

Important distinction:
- **Vapour barrier** — restricts movement of water vapour due to vapour pressure differentials
- **Air barrier** — restricts movement of air due to air pressure differentials

Also referred to as "vapour diffusion retarder."

## A-1.5.1.1.(1) — Application of Referenced Documents

Referenced documents may contain provisions covering a wide range of issues, including issues **unrelated** to the objectives and functional statements of the Code (e.g., aesthetic issues like colour-fastness). Such provisions are **excluded** from the Code's requirements.

This exclusion extends through the **entire chain of references** (secondary, tertiary referenced documents) — provisions unrelated to buildings or to the Code's objectives are not included, no matter how far down the chain they occur.
